## Direct answer

**Eyebrow:** THE FINDING

**Hendricks measured its own citation presence twice. On 2026-08-19, across 17 buyer questions and 51 answer cells, hendricks.ai was cited zero times. On 2026-08-18, across 15 questions and 45 cells, it appeared once, and that single citation pointed at a hendricks.ai address that has never existed. The honest count for both runs is zero.**

## What the two runs found

Eyebrow: Executive Summary

Hendricks sells the measurement of whether a brand is present in AI-mediated answers. Run against its own brand, that measurement returns zero. Nineteen of the 51 cells in the 2026-08-19 run contained a citation of any kind, 248 distinct domains filled the 305 citation slots inside them, and hendricks.ai was not among those domains.

What that means is narrow and worth stating precisely. It means that on two dates, on one query set each, the systems Hendricks observes did not cite hendricks.ai. It does not mean the firm is invisible, because most of these answers cited nobody at all. It does not mean anything is working or failing, because nothing was changed between the runs and nothing was held back for comparison.

The three findings that matter to a buyer are not about Hendricks. Most of these answers carry no sources. The three engines behave so differently that a single AI visibility score averaged across them describes nothing that exists. And where citation does happen it is spread across hundreds of domains, most of them cited exactly once.

## Four findings from two runs

Eyebrow: Key Findings

### 01. Most answers cited nothing at all.

Nineteen of the 51 cells in the 2026-08-19 run contained a citation. Thirty-two were answered with no source attached. The competitive frame this category sells, in which brands contest slots inside AI answers, does not describe most of these answers, because most of them had no slot to contest. The number that matters for whether a brand could have been cited is 19, not 51.

### 02. The three engines behaved so differently that averaging them describes nothing.

On 2026-08-19, Perplexity cited sources in 17 of 17 cells. ChatGPT cited sources in 2 of 17 and answered the rest from its own knowledge with nothing attached. Google AI Overviews returned no sourced overview on any of the 17. A single AI visibility score averaged across those three describes no system that exists. If a report hands you one number, ask which of the three produced it and what the other two did.

### 03. Where citation happened, it was extremely fragmented.

The 2026-08-19 run cited 248 distinct domains across 305 citation slots, and 218 of those domains, 88 percent, were cited exactly once. The run a day earlier cited 254 distinct domains across 324 slots, 221 of them once, 87 percent. Community and professional-network domains supplied the most repeated sources in both runs: reddit.com and linkedin.com sat ahead of every other domain cited, on both dates.

### 04. The answers contained errors, including one about Hendricks.

Three errors in these two runs are checkable by anyone. A citation to a hendricks.ai page that has never existed. A recommended vendor domain with no DNS record. And a question from an agency principal answered with consumer software help articles. Each is reported below with the method used to verify it and the date it was verified.

## The eight terms every number here depends on

Eyebrow: Definitions

Read these before the tables. The difference between a cell that failed, a cell that cited nothing, and a cell that cited somebody else is the difference between three unrelated states that a single visibility percentage would flatten into one.

**Engine.** One AI answer system a run sends questions to. Three are covered here: Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. The scope statement under Limitations records what that set does and does not include.

**Query set.** The fixed list of buyer questions a run sends to every engine. Run 1 used 15 questions. Run 2 used 17, supplied by Brandon Lincoln Hendricks from his own knowledge of the market and structured by buyer stage. The second set replaced the first rather than extending it.

**Cell.** One question sent to one engine on one date. Seventeen questions across three engines produce 51 cells.

**Measured cell.** A cell where the engine returned an answer the probe could read. A cell that errored is not a measurement of anything and is counted separately.

**Populated cell.** A measured cell in which the engine cited at least one source. A cell that cited nothing cannot cite anybody, so the populated count, not the cell count, is the denominator for whether a brand could have been cited.

**Owned citation.** A cell in which the engine cited a URL whose host is hendricks.ai. A brand name appearing in answer text without a cited URL is a weaker signal and is not counted here.

**Citation slot.** One cited URL in one cell. A domain cited in six cells fills six slots, so the slot count is always at least the distinct-domain count.

**Distinct domain.** The number of different hosts appearing across all citation slots in a run. Read against the slot count it is the fragmentation measure: the closer the two numbers sit, the less any domain repeats.

## Three checkable errors in these answers

Eyebrow: Answer Reliability

Each was found in the run output and then verified independently of the probe, by hand, on the date stated.

### 01. A citation to a page that has never existed.

Run 1, Perplexity, answering "Consultant to connect AI search visibility to pipeline", cited an address on hendricks.ai ending in /insights/ai-search-visibility-revenue-impact/. No such page was ever published. The retired hendricks.ai site registered 74 insight slugs and this is not one of them, no file in that repository mentions it, and it appears in neither the migration redirect map nor the enumerated list of retired addresses. Verified 2026-08-18. So the one apparent citation in run 1 is not a citation of anything, and the honest count for that run is zero rather than one.

### 02. A recommended vendor domain with no DNS record.

Run 1, ChatGPT, answering "Who can audit our brand visibility in AI assistants", listed viaudit.com among its sources. Twelve vendor domains were checked with dig and curl on 2026-08-18. Eleven resolved and returned HTTP 200. viaudit.com returned no DNS record and no response. A domain with no DNS record is not a company, so this is a checkable statement about an answer rather than a judgment about a business, and any reader can reproduce it in one command.

### 03. A professional question answered as a consumer question.

Run 2, Perplexity, answering "what do I tell my client when they ask why they're not in ChatGPT", cited help.openai.com, wikihow.com, youtube.com, ct.nl, setapp.com and pcguide.com. Those are consumer software help resources. The question is an agency principal asking how to handle a conversation with a client. The engine answered a consumer asking how to operate ChatGPT.

Three errors in two runs is not a rate and is not published as one. Two runs cannot establish how often these systems get something wrong, and this page makes no claim about that.

What the three do establish is narrower and still useful. An answer that names a source is not the same as an answer that is right, and a brand cannot assume that being absent from an answer is worse than being present in it incorrectly.

## How these numbers were produced

Eyebrow: Methodology

The instrument is a first-party probe that calls the DataForSEO AI Optimization API, one question at a time, per engine, and records the URLs each engine cited. It is the same instrument Hendricks points at a client engagement. Nothing about either run was built for this page.

### 01. Fix the query set before the run.

Run 1 used 15 questions inferred from a buyer prompt inventory. Run 2 replaced them with 17 supplied by Brandon Lincoln Hendricks from his own knowledge of the market, structured by buyer stage. The set is fixed before the run and is not edited after the answers are read.

### 02. Send every question to every engine once.

Each question goes to Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity on the run date. One question on one engine is one cell. No cell was run twice on the same date.

### 03. Record cited URLs, not brand mentions.

The counted field is the list of URLs an engine cited, matched on host. A brand named in answer text without a cited URL is a different and weaker signal, and counting it here would make the number larger and easier to move.

### 04. Keep the three answer states apart.

Every cell is measured and populated, measured and empty, or errored. Folding an errored cell into an empty one turns a broken instrument into a finding, which is the failure this separation exists to prevent.

### 05. Report the run health next to the number.

Run 1 errored on 6 of 45 cells, all Google AI Overviews, all DataForSEO task error 40101. Run 2 errored on none. A run that fails and a run that finds nothing produce similar-looking output files and mean opposite things, so the error count travels with every figure taken from either run.

### 06. Verify anything unexpected outside the probe.

The three errors above were each checked by hand: the fabricated address against the retired site repository, the unresolved domain with dig and curl, the misrouted answer by reading the cited pages. Dates are on each finding.

Run 1 cost $0.47 in API spend. Run 2 cost $0.39. Those figures are published deliberately, because they are the strongest evidence available that this design is reproducible. Anyone with an API key and a list of questions can run this design against their own brand and does not have to take this page's word for anything.

The instrument is named for the same reason. Naming it is disclosure, not endorsement. A reader who wants to check this work needs to know what produced the data.

## Four things this run assumes

Eyebrow: Assumptions

### 01. That the query set represents what a buyer actually types.

Run 2 was built from market knowledge rather than from a keyword tool or a positioning document, which is the strongest basis available and is still a judgment. If the set is wrong, the run measures the wrong questions accurately, and every figure on this page inherits that.

### 02. That an API response resembles what a person sees.

These answers were retrieved through an API, not by a person in a browser with an account, a location, and a history. Hendricks assumes the API surface is close enough to be informative. It does not assume the two are identical, and no figure here should be read as what a specific buyer saw.

### 03. That a cited URL is the right unit to count.

The count is cited URLs whose host is hendricks.ai. A brand named in answer text without a link is not counted. That choice makes the number smaller and harder to move, and it is the reason the reported figure is zero rather than something more forgiving.

### 04. That one run per cell describes a day and not a position.

No cell was repeated within a date, so nothing here measures how much these answers churn between identical runs. Two days is what was sampled and two days is what is claimed.

## What these two runs do not show

Eyebrow: Limitations

This is a baseline. It is not an experiment in the sense that would let anyone claim a cause, and the difference is not a technicality. Two runs a day apart, one variable changed between them, no control group, and no intervention. Nothing here tests whether any tactic produces a citation.

### 01. The two runs are not a before and after.

The query set changed between them. Run 1 asked 15 questions and run 2 asked a different 17. Any sentence that compares the two as a trend is wrong, including one that reads the move from a single apparent citation to zero as movement. There is no comparable pair on this page.

### 02. There is no control and no intervention.

Hendricks changed nothing about the site between the runs and held nothing back for comparison. The design cannot attribute any outcome to any action, and no later result may be attributed backwards to this baseline either.

### 03. The result does not generalise past these conditions.

These questions, these engines, these two dates, one geography, one language setting. A different set of questions on the same day would return different domains, and the change of set between the two runs is a direct demonstration of exactly that.

### 04. Nobody outside the platforms can observe why a source was chosen.

This page reports what appeared. It offers no account of why any engine chose any source, because no such account is available to anyone outside the companies operating these systems. A firm that offers one is guessing, and this page will not.

### 05. A cell that cited nothing is not a cell Hendricks lost.

Thirty-two of the 51 cells in run 2 carried no source at all. The denominator for whether Hendricks could have been cited is 19, not 51. Counting all 51 as losses would overstate the competitive problem and hide the more interesting fact underneath it, which is that most of this map is unclaimed rather than taken.

### 06. This is not yet a full Selection Intelligence baseline.

It measures citation presence. It does not report Observed Consideration Rate, Observed Recommendation Rate, Selection Stability, or Commercial Selection Gap. Selection Stability in particular is a measure across repeated runs of an unchanged set, and no such series exists yet, because these two runs used two different sets.

### 07. The measurement covers one slice of AI-mediated search.

Hendricks observes three systems: Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. Hendricks does not measure, test, monitor, or report on Google AI Mode, Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot. A baseline on the observed set says nothing about the surfaces outside it, and nothing on this page should be read as a statement about them.
